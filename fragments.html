Fragments

Unpublished pieces of writing, in different forms.

It's not about meaning, purpose, or achievement. It's about experience — to taste, to touch, to see, to feel.

This human existence is nothing but a dance of sensations, a fleeting moment between birth and dissolution.

And yet, we must ground ourselves or risk dissolving into the infinite.

Maybe the purpose isn't to transcend, but simply to be. Lose yourself in a song, in a sunset, in a breath, in holding someone's hand.

kindness begins
when we see wounds
instead of enemies.

we are free when we are ourselves.
we are ourselves when we stop searching outside.

our true self was always there.
it never needed to be created,
only remembered.

nothing is missing.
nothing is created.
everything is already within.

peel the layers. undo the conditioning. remember who you have always been.

my sleeves match the flowers. i am one of them. i am a flower.

here to bloom. some are very similar, while others are different, each has its own beauty, each opens at its own pace, each thrives in different climates, environments, light, or shadow.

together, they create diversity. through it nature sustains itself: different insects feeding from them, different trees growing around them, and different fruits emerging from what disappear.

do we think one flower is worth more than another?

do we think a flower is weak because it blooms only once a year and then rests?

do we think difference is a problem?

no, we don't.

so why would we think that about ourselves or one another?

let's cherish humans as we cherish flowers. let's be amazed by our differences, our seasons, our periods of rest, and our blooming.

my hands on the piano
our bodies lingering for hours
friends dancing and laughing around
the tree of my dreams in the background
the infinite starry sky

what i’ll miss the most
is not you or the walls
but how the world opened
in those moments

The Masquerade

We are expected to be “aligned” but always efficient,
“vulnerable” but still stylish,
“real” but always likable,
“soft” but productive,
“spiritual” but sexy,
“rebellious” but well-behaved,
“at peace” but not too quiet,
“unapologetic” but perfectly marketed.

We can no longer simply be lost, we have to be “in transition.”
We can no longer say, “I’m hurting,” we say, “I’m processing something.”
We can no longer simply cry, we have to be “going through an emotional release.”
We can no longer just put our phones down, we have to “do a conscious digital detox.”
We can no longer simply doubt, we are “going through a recalibration phase.”

Even our chaos speaks the right language.
Even our chaos has learned to stand straight.

Do you see the paradox?
We talk about authenticity, but we perform it.
We talk about freedom, but we script it.
We talk about truth, but we filter it through a golden light.

The great social theatre has simply changed its costume.

We traded the suit, the tie, the stilettos, for a bohemian outfit: linen shirts, crystals around our necks, and delicate tattoos.
We no longer talk about ambition, we talk about “listening to ourselves,” “connection,” “the right vibration.”
We no longer sell performance, we sell presence, authenticity, unconditional love.
But this new social character, sometimes, too often, is still a perfectly polished mask.

Welcome to the masquerade.
An open-air theatre where everyone plays a role they never truly chose, where the script changes depending on the room, and where the spotlight is always turned toward what others might think.

Excerpt from a book project · spiritual satire · raw psychology · uncomfortable truths

I was walking on the pavement, looking at nothing. My eyes daydreaming, as they often do.

Something caught my peripheral vision. Three little purple shapes on the concrete.

I thought they were flowers. Soft flowers. The kind you love. Maybe the kind you are.

I focused. My blurry, dreaming eyes slowly came back. I stared at the three purple shapes.

No flowers. Just stains.
